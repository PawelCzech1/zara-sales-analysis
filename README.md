
# **Sales Analysis and Customer Purchasing Behavior at ZARA – Excel Dashboard**  
**version:** 1.0.0  
**available languages:** [English] | Polish  

This project demonstrates how I used Power Query and Excel to perform a sales analysis, seasonality analysis, and an assessment of how promotions influence customer behavior in Zara stores.

**Data source:** data obtained from kaggle.com  

---

## **Summary**

The analysis focuses on the impact of seasonality on sales, the extent of promotional activity across products, and how product placement within the store affects sales performance.

---

## **Business Context / Key Questions**

1. Which product categories generate the highest revenue  
2. Do promotional products generate more revenue than non‑promotional ones  
3. How does product placement in the store influence sales  
4. Which products are the top performers in terms of revenue  
5. Do seasonal products sell better than non‑seasonal ones  

---

## **Tools and Techniques Used**

- **Power Query** – data cleaning and transformation (ETL)  
- **Excel** – main tool for analysis and visualization  
- **Pivot tables and pivot charts** – data aggregation and interactive visualizations  
- **Slicers** – filtering results by product type  
- **Charts:**  
  - KPI tiles (total revenue, total sales volume, number of product categories)  
  - line chart – comparison of seasonal vs non‑seasonal product sales over time  
  - bar chart – sales of promotional vs non‑promotional products by category  
  - combo chart – revenue by category and percentage contribution to total revenue  
- **Dashboard UX** – two thematic pages, KPI tiles at the top, slicer placed prominently for user convenience  

---

## **Key Insights**

- **Revenue structure**  
  - Total revenue amounts to USD 28.01M, generated from 31,473 transactions.  
  - Average revenue per transaction is approximately USD 890.

- **Revenue concentration by category**  
  - The *Jackets* category accounts for roughly 60–65% of total revenue.  
  - Revenue from this category is more than five times higher than the second‑largest category.  
  - The remaining four categories together generate 35–40% of revenue.  
  - **Conclusion:** revenue is heavily concentrated in one dominant category.

- **Volume analysis – best‑selling products**  
  - The top 10 products each reach between 2,000 and 12,000 units sold.

---

## **Recommendations**

- Priority should be given to further optimizing the *Jackets* category, as it generates the largest share of revenue and has the strongest impact on overall performance.  
- Strengthening the remaining categories could reduce dependency on a single segment and improve revenue stability.  
- The best‑selling products (Top 10) show strong potential — increasing their availability could quickly boost sales volume.  
- A seasonality analysis is recommended, as dominance of one category may indicate demand fluctuations across different periods.  
- Implementing ongoing KPI monitoring (category share, volume, average revenue) would support faster reaction to changes in sales trends.
